Shorter week — pushed through Days 59, 60, and 62 of the 100 Days of Code challenge. This stretch tied together a bunch of recent work and finally got into CMake.

## What I built

Day 59 was a CLI word frequency counter that reads text files and dumps results as JSON. Pulled together the argument parsing and nlohmann/json stuff from the previous week into something actually useful. Day 60 took that same tool and made it multi-threaded — split files across worker threads and merged the frequency maps at the end. That was the milestone project I'd been wanting to build: thread pool + file I/O + JSON output all in one place.

Day 62 was CMake in depth — proper targets, building a static library, and understanding `PUBLIC` vs `PRIVATE` linking. Long overdue.

## What I learned

- Going from single-threaded to multi-threaded on the word counter was a good exercise in figuring out what actually needs synchronization. Merging frequency maps after the threads finish was simpler and cleaner than trying to share a map with locks.
- CMake finally makes sense now that I have a project with enough pieces to justify it. Targets and linking visibility (`PUBLIC`/`PRIVATE`) clicked once I stopped thinking of CMake as a config file and started thinking of it as describing dependency relationships.

- Want to keep scaling up project complexity now that the tooling supports it.
