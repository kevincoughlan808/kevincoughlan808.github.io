Solid week — pushed through Days 75–77 of the 100 Days of Code challenge. Still in the SFML graphics lane, and things are starting to feel like actual game development.

## What I built

Day 75 was the delta time day I saw coming — proper frame-rate independent movement using a game loop with `dt`. After noticing last week that fixed-offset movement only works at a locked framerate, this felt like the natural next step. Day 76 was collision detection — AABB and circle-circle, both visualized with SFML so you can see the hits register. Day 77 was a particle system with click-to-emit, fade, and lifetime. That one pulled together a surprising amount — spawn particles on mouse click, update positions each frame, fade alpha over the lifetime, and remove dead particles.

## What I learned

- Delta time is one of those concepts that sounds trivial until you wire it up and realize your movement code was subtly wrong before. Multiplying by `dt` everywhere is simple but it changes how you think about every update.
- AABB vs circle collision was a good exercise in thinking about tradeoffs — AABB is cheaper but circles feel more natural for a lot of cases. Implementing both side by side made the differences concrete.
- The particle system was the most satisfying thing I've built in the SFML stretch so far. Managing a vector of particles with lifetimes and fading felt like a real gameplay system, not just a demo.
