Solid week — pushed through Days 67–70 of the 100 Days of Code challenge. Stayed in the performance and tooling lane from last week and went deeper.

## What I built

Day 67 was a benchmarking harness using `<chrono>` — built a reusable timing setup and then dug into dead code elimination, where the compiler just optimizes away the thing you're trying to benchmark. Had to learn the hard way why `benchmark::DoNotOptimize` exists.

Day 68 was sanitizers — ASan, UBSan, and TSan demos. Similar energy to the AddressSanitizer day back in the memory management stretch, but this time covering undefined behavior and thread sanitizers too. Day 69 shifted into cross-platform builds — conditional compilation with preprocessor macros and writing a CMake toolchain file.

Day 70 was matrix multiplication — naive triple-loop vs cache-tiled, with benchmarks to compare. Another cache locality exercise, but this time the speedup from tiling was dramatic enough to make the point stick.

## What I learned

- Dead code elimination is one of those things that makes you question every benchmark you've ever written. If you're not careful the compiler just deletes your work and you're timing nothing.
- TSan catching a race condition I didn't think I had was a good reminder that concurrency bugs hide well. The sanitizer stretch pairs nicely with the threading work from a few weeks ago.
- Cache-tiled matrix multiply drove home the same lesson as the AoS vs SoA benchmark — memory access patterns matter more than I instinctively think they do.
