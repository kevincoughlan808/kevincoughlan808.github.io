Solid week — pushed through Days 93–95 of the 100 Days of Code challenge. Built Conway's Game of Life from scratch, and it came together faster than expected.

## What I built

Day 93 was the grid data structure — a 2D grid with a neighbor count function that wraps at the edges. Wrapping was the right call over bounded edges; it makes the simulation way more interesting when gliders and patterns can loop around. Day 94 was the update logic — classic copy-then-apply so you're not mutating the grid mid-tick. Day 95 brought it to life with SFML rendering, pause/resume, and click-to-toggle cells. Being able to pause, draw a pattern, and unpause to watch it evolve is the kind of interactivity that makes these projects feel real.

## What I learned

- Copy-then-apply is one of those things that's obvious once you think about it but easy to get wrong if you rush. Updating cells in place means earlier updates contaminate later neighbor counts. Bit me once before I caught it.
- The wrapping neighbor count was a good excuse to use modular arithmetic properly. Cleaner than boundary checks everywhere.
- This was the fastest SFML project to get running, which I think says something about how much the earlier SFML work paid off. Grid rendering, input handling, game loop — all felt routine at this point.
