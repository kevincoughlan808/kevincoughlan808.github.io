Solid week to close out the challenge. Days 96–100 were about taking a working project and doing all the things you skip when you're just trying to get something running — tests, profiling, sanitizers, docs, and a proper release tag.

## What I built

Day 96 was a GTest suite covering `Grid` and `UpdateSystem`. Writing the update test caught a mistake I'd have missed — I'd assumed (2,2) would stay dead, but it has three live neighbours after one step, so it should come alive. The test flagged it before I even ran the game.

Day 97 was a performance pass. Profiled with `std::chrono` timers around the update call, got a baseline of ~3.2ms per generation, then swapped to a double-buffer approach — two grids that persist for the life of the program, writing into the second and swapping instead of allocating a new grid every tick. Brought it down to ~2.5ms, about a 22% improvement.

Day 98 was ASan + UBSan — compiled with `-fsanitize=address,undefined`, ran the game, and got a clean run. No memory errors, no undefined behaviour. Reassuring rather than exciting, which is exactly what you want.

Day 99 was the README — rewrote it from scratch as a proper user-facing doc with dependencies, build steps, run commands, and controls. Day 100 was tagging v1.0.0 and pushing it.

## What I learned

- Double-buffering is the kind of optimisation that's obvious once you see it, but you have to profile first to know it matters. Measuring before changing is the habit.
- ASan and UBSan are easy to add and give you genuine confidence. Should have been using them throughout.
- Writing tests after the fact is better than not writing them at all, but writing them first (Day 88's TDD session) was noticeably smoother. The test for the update rules made me think more carefully about the expected state than I had when writing the code.
- 100 days is a long time. The capstone pulled together design patterns, ECS, CMake, CI, testing, profiling, and sanitizers — all things that felt abstract when I first hit them.
